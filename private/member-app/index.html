The foundations of movement

Stances & ready posture

A common Wado reference: recognise stance names and ready posture, then follow your instructor's demonstration. This is not an exhaustive list.

Formal & natural

Closed and open standing positions. Shizentai describes natural posture here, rather than an extra fixed stance.

01

Musubi-dachi

Formal attention stance

Heels together, toes opening out.

Used when coming to attention and bowing.

02

Heisoku-dachi

Feet-together stance

Feet together, with toes and heels close.

A closed position found in kata.

03

Heiko-dachi

Parallel stance

Feet apart and parallel.

A natural standing base; follow the version shown in class.

04

Hachiji-dachi

Open natural stance / Shizentai

Feet apart, toes opening out.

A natural ready position before or between movements.

Forward stances

Recognise the relationship between the forward leg and punching hand.

05

Junzuki-dachi

順突き立ち - Front Punch Stance

Simple English: A stance used for a punch with the same hand and leg forward, for example left leg forward with a left punch.

Practice note: Follow the foot placement, stance length and weight distribution demonstrated by your instructor. Use this reference to recognise the stance, then refine it in class.

Used for: Junzuki and the fundamental blocks shown from Junzuki-dachi in your syllabus.
06

Gyakuzuki-dachi

逆突き立ち - Reverse Punch Stance

Simple English: A stance used for a reverse punch: the punching hand is opposite the forward leg.

Practice note: Practise the stance and hip action together as demonstrated in class. Your instructor will help you with foot position, stance length and balance.

Used for: Reverse punches (gyakuzuki)

Cat stances

Neko-ashi variants differ in how the body faces. Let your instructor demonstrate their placement and balance.

07

Shomen Neko-ashi-dachi

Front-facing cat stance

Body facing forwards, front foot lightly placed.

Recognise the front-facing member of the cat-stance family in kata.

08

Hanmi Neko-ashi-dachi

Half-body cat stance

Body partly side-on, front foot lightly placed.

A cat stance with a partly turned body; not the same term as Hanmi-gamae.

09

Mahanmi Neko-ashi-dachi

Side-facing cat stance

Body more fully side-on, front foot lightly placed.

Recognise its more side-facing posture within the cat-stance family.

Kata bases

Reference shapes you may meet in kata. These reminders do not prescribe stance depth or movement.

10

Shiko-dachi

Open, bent-knee stance

Feet apart, toes turned out, knees bent.

An open base used in kata.

11

Naihanchi-dachi

Naihanchi stance

Feet apart with toes turned slightly inward.

The characteristic base associated with Naihanchi kata.

12

Seishan-dachi

Hourglass stance family

An inward-set base with an hourglass shape.

The family includes yoko and tate forms; follow the version demonstrated in class.

13

Kosa-dachi

Crossed stance

The legs cross, bringing one foot across the other.

A crossed base found in kata, including Wanshu.

14

Kokutsu-dachi

Back stance

A front-and-back base with the rear knee bent.

Recognise it as a back stance; follow the Wado form shown in class.

Ready guard

A guard describes readiness and body position, not one fixed foot base.

15

Hanmi-gamae

半身構え - Half-body ready guard

Simple English: Natural fighting stance, ready position.

Practice note: A ready posture with the body turned partly sideways. Follow your instructor's demonstration of foot placement, hand position and movement.

Migi Hanmi-gamae: Right leg forward.

Hidari Hanmi-gamae: Left leg forward.

Used for: The strikes and kicks marked as starting from Hanmi-gamae in your syllabus.
Keep in mind: Junzuki and gyakuzuki describe the relationship between the punching hand and forward leg. Hanmi-gamae is a guard or ready posture, not one fixed stance. Ask your instructor about stance length, balance and movement.

Background Wado references: US Eastern Wado terminology and the WIKF Belgium handbook (PDF). These are reference sources, not a claim of Hakuba, BWF or IWF approval.

Before you train

Belt tying

A reminder of the belt-tying sequence for home practice. Ask your instructor to demonstrate any step you are unsure about.

Important: The way you tie your belt demonstrates discipline, respect, and attention to detail - core values of Wado Ryu karate. Take time to learn this properly.

Step-by-Step Instructions

  1. Find the Centre
    Fold your belt in half to find the middle point. Hold this centre point against your abdomen, around belly button height.
  2. Wrap Around
    Wrap both ends of the belt around your waist to the back, keeping the belt flat against your body. The ends should be roughly equal length when they meet at the back.
  3. Bring to Front
    Bring both ends back to the front. Cross the right end over the left end in front of your body.
  4. Tuck Under
    Take the right end and tuck it under BOTH layers of the belt (between your gi and the belt), pulling it up through the centre. This is the key step that distinguishes Hon-Musubi.
  5. Create the Knot
    Now take the top end (that you just pulled through) and place it over the bottom end, forming a loop. Thread the top end through this loop from above.
  6. Tighten
    Pull both ends evenly to tighten the knot. The knot should sit flat and square against your abdomen. Both ends should hang down evenly, roughly the same length.
Practice Tip: The finished knot should be a square knot (koma-musubi) that sits flat and neat. Both ends should hang at roughly the same length. Practice this daily until it becomes automatic!

General Terms

Dojo (道場)
Training hall - "The place of the way". We bow entering and exiting to show respect.
Sensei (先生)
Teacher - Literally "preceding in life". Used for any teacher, not just martial arts.
Kata (形)
Form - Pre-arranged sequence of movements
Kihon (基本)
Basics - Fundamental techniques
Kumite (組手)
Sparring - Literally "grappling hands". Partner practice.
Waza (技)
Technique - A specific move or skill
Uke (受け)
Block - Literally "to receive". Defensive techniques.
Kiai (気合)
Spirit shout - Literally "unification of energy". Used to focus effort at the moment of impact.
Ki (氣)
Energy/Spirit - Internal energy cultivated through training and proper technique.
Bunkai (分解)
Application - Literally "to break down". Practical application of kata movements.
Gi (着)
Uniform - The karate training suit
Obi (帯)
Belt - Worn around the waist to indicate rank
